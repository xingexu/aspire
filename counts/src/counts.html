Our answer to the Aspire 2026 case · Initiative: PD USC

Everything you do at Western should count.

Counts is a PD USC program. Clubs keep their brand and run the events students already like. The USC adds reach, a small payment for every new face, and proof students can take into an interview. The path ends at Council.

The problem

24.6%

turnout in the February 2026 USC election, down from 34.6% in 2023.

16 of 51

faculty and affiliate seats on Council were vacant in early October 2026.

A few

familiar places are where most students stay. They rarely reach the full range of what the USC offers.

Why it matters

The USC isn’t short of offerings. Students don’t see a reason to step in, so fewer of them ever get close to running for Council.

Why it matters

Students give their time to whatever helps them get hired. PD USC exists for exactly that, and the case lists it among the USC programs students underuse.

Source: Aspire 2026 case, Table 1

Clubs have the brands. The USC has the reach.

Clubs

  • 240+ clubs reach 17,000+ students a year.
  • Students’ sense of belonging sits in smaller communities like these.
  • Promotion is each club’s own job, and a club needs 30 paid members and four events a year to stay ratified.

The USC

  • The Spoke, The Wave and the USC Now app reach students every day.
  • It approves every club event in WesternLink, 3 to 10 business days ahead, so it already sees what’s coming.
  • It can confirm what a student actually did. A club poster can’t.
Why it matters

Today the two don’t trade. The USC funds clubs through a Grant Fund that takes about three weeks to decide and won’t pay for regular events. Counts makes the trade one checkbox.

Sources: USC Club Executive Manual; USC Clubs Event Proposal How-To; USC Grant Fund application

How it works

  1. A club makes its event count.One box on the WesternLink proposal it already files, plus three promises: open to everyone, free, and someone at the door.
  2. The USC promotes it.The event goes into the USC Now feed and onto the screens at The Spoke and The Wave.
  3. Students check in and climb.A door code confirms the visit. Each student new to the club earns it $3 from employer partners, and every step goes on the student’s record.

From showing up to Council

1 · Show upCheck in at a club you haven’t been to. Confirmed by the door code.
2 · Pitch inGreet, keep time or set up. Confirmed by the club president.
3 · LeadRun an event or join an executive team. Confirmed by the club.
4 · RepresentPolicy Fellows or a run for Council. With the USC advocacy team.

Policy Fellows

Each term, 10 to 15 students work with the USC’s advocacy team (an executive, an associate VP and three coordinators) on one issue: research, a one-page brief, one meeting with a city councillor or MPP, and a presentation to Council. This term’s issue is youth jobs.

Run for It

Campaign skills taught as professional development before the by-elections and the February election: public speaking, budgeting and persuasion. Several faculty president races went uncontested in 2026.

Why it matters

The case asks for ways to bolster interest in student government. Every rung is a real professional skill, so the ladder gives students a career reason to climb toward Council. The Grant Fund can’t pay for advocacy, so the USC’s own advocacy team runs the top rung.

Sources: Aspire 2026 case; USC Grant Fund application

Why it matters

Western’s Co-Curricular Record already exists, but students have to write reflections and find a staff validator, and it lists 482 approved activities. Counts records each step at the door and would feed that record, not replace it.
